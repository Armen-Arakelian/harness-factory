# Review Saved Conclusions

Resolve the project root three directories above this skill folder. Read its `rules.md`, especially User Conclusions in Research and Reading and Research. Use this skill on request, not after every answer or save. A full review is broader than ordinary recall, but is not a requirement to load the entire vault at once.

## Establish Scope

List the conclusion files. For a full review, consider every saved conclusion; for a requested subset, keep that scope explicit while consulting other conclusions when relevant. Read the selected thoughts in full, in manageable batches. Recover truncated sections rather than assume they were examined. If none exist, say so and stop. Do not create example thoughts to supply a review result.

Preserve the actual wording, ownership, scope and uncertainty. Distinguish factual claims from personal positions, preferences and tentative ideas. Lack of proof does not make a personal thought wrong. Do not assume the user has changed their mind because another note is newer; dates alone do not resolve contradictory thoughts.

## Search Related Memory Deeply

For each thought or related group, search saved `nodes/`, `people/` and `details/`, and other relevant conclusions. Search titles and content using subjects, aliases, synonyms and terms for possible exceptions or opposing claims. The README map and outgoing links are useful entry points, not the search boundary: actively look for relevant unlinked records. Group genuinely overlapping searches to avoid reading the same evidence repeatedly.

Read matching short notes. Inspect deeper material by headings and useful passages, including its methods, sources, dates, qualifiers and limitations. Expand to adjacent sections, related concepts or additional files when necessary to assess a conflict; use no fixed graph depth or file quota. Do not stop at a summary or first supporting example when the conclusion makes a broader claim. A short relevant detail can be read fully. Keep unrelated material out of context.

Relevant agent insights may point to evidence, but are not independent proof. A person's statement is attributed testimony, not automatically a verified fact; an employer or contact status supplies no evidential authority. Repeated notes based on one underlying source are not independent corroboration. Do not use drafts in `work/`, glossary definitions, missing files or a memory summary as substitutes for supporting evidence.

This review uses saved project material. Do not browse externally, start new experiments or fabricate missing evidence to finish it. If current validity requires new information, identify the gap rather than declare the claim false or current. Report incomplete coverage when relevant files could not be read; do not label a partial review complete.

## Evaluate Without Manufacturing Findings

Look for a concrete contradiction, an overgeneralization beyond recorded evidence, a factual caveat that changes interpretation, or an apparent duplicate whose meaning and scope really overlap. Cite the conclusion and the specific conflicting or qualifying saved passage when raising an issue. Check whether different contexts, time periods, meanings or explicitly tentative positions explain an apparent disagreement. Preserve unresolved alternatives when the record cannot decide between them.

There is no minimum number of findings and no obligation to suggest changes. Do not invent objections, demand arbitrary precision, flag stylistic preferences as substantive problems or reword a sound thought simply to produce output. Similar wording alone does not justify merging distinct perspectives; a consolidation suggestion is optional and must explain a real benefit.

Keep these outcomes separate:

- **No grounded concern found:** the checked material revealed no problem worth raising. This does not establish that all claims are true or proven.
- **Insufficient evidence:** the project lacks the information needed to assess a material claim, or the saved evidence cannot resolve the issue. Missing support alone is not contradictory evidence.
- **Grounded concern:** the saved record supplies a specific conflict, unsupported scope or other substantive reason for attention.

## Respond and Leave Memory Unchanged

Give a concise result. A clean review can be one line: "I found no problems in the saved material checked; I suggest no changes." If assessment is not possible, say what could not be determined and the important missing information. For mixed results, report only grounded issues and material assessment gaps with note links, brief reasons and an optional direction for revision. Do not print a catalogue of unchanged conclusions or a large report unless requested. No extra review artifact is created automatically.

The review edits nothing: conclusions, information, people, details, README, topics, glossary, links, settings and agent memory remain unchanged. No checkpoint, obsolete flag or automatic replacement is authorized. The user chooses follow-up changes. Then `../update-memory/SKILL.md` presents precise edits, or explicit merge/deletion operations, through the normal one-use approval workflow. Selecting an issue to discuss is not approval to apply unspecified wording. Do not initiate a new user conclusion of your own.
